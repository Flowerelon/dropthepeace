## 1.1 Autonomous Weapons (LAWS -- Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems)

**Definition:** Lethal autonomous weapon systems are weapons that use sensor suites and computer algorithms to independently identify a target and employ an onboard weapon system to engage and destroy it without manual human control.

**Key facts and developments:**

- In **December 2024**, the UN General Assembly adopted a resolution on LAWS with overwhelming support: **166 votes in favor, 3 opposed, 15 abstentions**, mandating new international consultations to advance negotiations on a treaty or agreement to regulate autonomous weapons systems.
- The **UN Secretary-General** has recommended that States conclude, by **2026**, a legally binding instrument to prohibit LAWS that function without human control or oversight.
- In **May 2025**, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called for a global ban on "killer robots," describing them as "politically unacceptable, morally repugnant."
- At least **30 countries** already have defensive autonomous weapons that operate under human supervision. Around the globe, militaries are racing to build robotic weapons with increasing autonomy.
- More than **120 countries** support calls to negotiate a treaty that prohibits and regulates autonomous weapons systems.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Removes the psychological and moral cost of killing from the aggressor
- Makes warfare cheaper and more accessible
- Creates accountability gaps: who is responsible when an autonomous system kills civilians?
- Lowers the political cost of military action (fewer body bags to explain)

## 1.2 AI in Cyberwarfare

**Key facts and developments:**

- Nation-states are increasingly leveraging AI to launch more potent, elusive, and autonomous cyberattacks, fundamentally altering the landscape of military cyber operations.
- The **cyber warfare market** is projected to reach **USD 66.84 billion by 2035**, driven by AI-powered offensive and defensive capabilities.
- **China's** cyber doctrine prioritizes "information dominance" -- AI, big-data fusion, and quantum R&D are explicitly directed at compressing the OODA loop and enabling pre-emptive cyber strikes.
- **Russia's** AI-powered cyberattacks are assessed as threatening to outpace Western defenses (FDD, February 2025).
- In **2025**, U.S. National Security Council cybersecurity official Alexei Bulazel called to "destigmatize" offensive cyber operations (OCO) at the RSA conference, framing them as vital tools for competing with foreign adversaries.
- Civilian infrastructure -- hospitals, power grids, smart devices -- is increasingly targeted using AI-powered cyber warfare to create chaos and gain geopolitical leverage.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- AI makes cyberattacks faster, more scalable, and harder to attribute
- AI-generated malware can mutate and adapt to defenses in real time
- Blurs the line between peace and war (cyberattacks can be devastating without a single shot fired)
- Attribution problems mean states can attack without consequences
- Critical infrastructure attacks can cause real-world casualties

## 1.3 AI-Powered Disinformation and Propaganda

**Key facts and developments:**

- The **World Economic Forum** ranked AI-driven disinformation as the **largest short-term threat to civil society** in both 2024 and 2025.
- Around **500,000 deepfake videos** were shared on social media in 2023, with projections showing up to **8 million by 2025** -- a 1,500% increase in two years.
- **Romania's 2024 presidential election** was annulled after evidence of AI-powered interference using manipulated videos.
- **Ireland's 2025 presidential election** saw a deepfake video falsely depicting the eventual winner withdrawing his candidature.
- In **January 2024**, fraudsters used deepfake technology to impersonate a company CFO on a video call, tricking an employee into transferring **$25 million**.
- Survey data across eight countries shows prior exposure to deepfakes **increases belief in misinformation** (the "illusory truth effect").
- **86% of AI researchers** are significantly or extremely concerned about AI and the spread of false information; **79%** about manipulation of large-scale public opinion trends.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Can manufacture public support for military action based on fabricated events
- Can destabilize governments and create internal chaos
- Can impersonate leaders to create diplomatic crises
- Erodes trust in all media, making truth impossible to establish
- Can be weaponized by state actors to justify aggression

## 1.4 AI in Surveillance and Social Control

**Key facts and developments:**

- **China** is using AI to turbocharge surveillance and control of 1.4 billion citizens. AI tools "automate censorship, enhance surveillance, and pre-emptively suppress dissent."
- Since **2025**, multiple Chinese institutions have pursued AI systems that capitalize on information sourced by "grid workers" -- community-level monitors who survey assigned neighborhoods and report to authorities.
- By **2025**, an estimated **90% of data feeding into China's Social Credit System** is sourced from AI surveillance technologies.
- By **2027**, **over 100 countries** may adopt China's behavioral scoring technology.
- **Pegasus spyware** (Israeli NSO Group) infiltrates smartphones without any user interaction (zero-click). In **May 2025**, a US court ordered NSO Group to pay **$167 million in damages** to Meta.
- Harvard research documents algorithmic surveillance and digital occupation using Pegasus in **Kashmir and Palestine**.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Mass surveillance enables authoritarian control that removes dissent and makes war decisions unilateral
- Exported surveillance tech creates client-state dependencies
- Surveillance of foreign citizens and diplomats creates diplomatic crises
- Predictive policing models can be adapted for pre-emptive military targeting
- Social credit systems can be extended to control populations in occupied territories

## 1.5 AI Arms Race Between Nations (US, China, Russia)

**Key facts and developments:**

- **US private AI investment in 2024:** approximately **$109.1 billion** -- nearly 12x China's $9.3 billion in private investment.
- **China plans $98 billion** in AI investments (including $56 billion from government sources) amid escalating tech rivalry.
- The US has about **5,426 data centers** as of March 2025; China has **449 facilities**. Together they account for **70% of global data center capacity**.
- **Russia's** Military-Industrial Commission has approved plans to derive **30% of combat power from remote controlled and AI-enabled robotic platforms by 2030**.
- Vladimir Putin stated: **"The leader in AI will rule the world."**
- Under the **second Trump administration (2025)**, the US began a broad deregulation campaign to accelerate AI growth, including nuclear energy, infrastructure, and high-performance computing.
- Competition might lead to heightened tensions **reminiscent of the nuclear arms race**, such that the quest for superiority risks precipitating, rather than deterring, conflict.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Mirrors Cold War nuclear arms race dynamics
- Creates first-mover advantages that incentivize preemptive action
- Export controls (e.g., US chip restrictions on China) create economic warfare
- Technological superiority tempts nations to act while they still have an edge
- No equivalent of nuclear deterrence (MAD) exists for AI weapons

## 1.6 AI Decision-Making in Military Contexts (Speed, Removing Humans from the Loop)

**Key facts and developments:**

- AI systems can process information and make tactical adjustments in **fractions of a second**, far exceeding human reaction times.
- The **November 2024 agreement** between Xi Jinping and Joe Biden affirmed that AI must never supplant human judgment in nuclear weapon launch authorization.
- However, experts warn that "human-in-the-loop" is **insufficient**. Automation bias -- the tendency to over-trust machines under crisis conditions -- could amplify errors and produce tightly coupled feedback loops.
- Current U.S. policy maintains a "human in the loop" for nuclear decisions, but AI tools are entering nuclear command, control, and communications **faster than diplomacy can react**.
- In a **simulation study**, AI models overwhelmingly escalated toward nuclear options, choosing tactical nuclear action in **95% of scenarios** and rarely opting for de-escalation.
- AI compresses the **OODA loop** (Observe, Orient, Decide, Act) to near-instantaneous levels, narrowing the window for diplomacy and de-escalation.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Speed of AI decision-making may outpace human ability to intervene
- Creates pressure to delegate authority to machines or risk losing in the speed competition
- Automation bias means humans rubber-stamp machine decisions
- Compressed timelines leave no room for diplomacy
- Errors propagate faster than they can be detected

## 1.7 AI Lowering the Barrier to Conflict

**Key facts and developments:**

- **Open-source and commercial AI models** can be repurposed easily for military uses, lowering barriers to entry for non-state actors including terrorist organizations.
- AI lowers barriers for the development of **nuclear and bioweapons, automated hacking tools, and AI-optimized malware**, allowing hostile nations and terrorist groups to build unconventional weapons with minimal resources.
- Many AI services including LLMs are **free or open-source** with intuitive interfaces, making certain applications extremely accessible.
- **Islamic State Khorasan** has intensified media capabilities; Islamic State released a guide in **2023** on how to securely use generative AI.
- The **U.S. intelligence community** elevated AI as a **top global threat** in its March 2026 annual threat assessment report.
- AI lowers the **threshold for the use of force**, making wars more politically and operationally feasible, and compresses decision time, narrowing the window for diplomacy.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Democratizes access to sophisticated weapons and attack capabilities
- Non-state actors can now wage asymmetric warfare with AI
- Lowers the economic cost of conflict
- Makes it easier to initiate hostilities without large standing armies
- Terrorist groups gain force-multiplier capabilities

## 1.8 AI in Nuclear Command and Control

**Key facts and developments:**

- AI is entering nuclear command, control, and communications (NC3) **faster than diplomacy can react**.
- AI capabilities could be destabilizing -- for example, **AI-enabled tracking and targeting of adversary nuclear launchers** could pressure an adversary into a preemptive first strike or into expanding its arsenal.
- SIPRI's June 2025 paper explores whether AI introduces new risks, exacerbates existing ones, or fundamentally alters the nature of nuclear escalation.
- The **UN adopted its first resolution on AI in nuclear command and control in 2025**.
- The "AI-induced transparency paradox": AI might make adversary nuclear forces more visible, which paradoxically could be destabilizing if it undermines second-strike capabilities.
- In **July 2025**, the U.S. Department of Defense requested funds from Congress to add novel technologies to nuclear command, control, and communications.

## 1.10 Algorithmic Bias in Threat Assessment

**Key facts and developments:**

- The **ICRC (International Committee of the Red Cross)** published two major papers in 2024 on algorithmic bias in military AI systems (March and September 2024).
- **SIPRI** published a background paper on bias in military AI in December 2024.
- **Project Maven's** algorithms could correctly identify a tank in good weather only about **60% of the time**, dropping to **30% accuracy in snowy conditions**.
- "Kill lists" generated by AI depend on data inputs that conform to preexisting societal biases, likely containing racial and identity stereotypes.
- AI-driven counterterrorism systems **focus excessively on regions associated with specific religions or ethnic groups**, disproportionately affecting marginalized communities.
- Proprietary algorithms are opaque -- the military cannot easily inspect targeting algorithms for hidden biases that misidentify civilians as combatants.
- Algorithmic bias "poses a greater challenge to autonomous weapons systems developers than most other risks" because it takes longer to rectify a discriminatory algorithm than to issue an apology for an occasional mistake.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Biased threat assessments can target innocent populations, creating cycles of retaliation
- Structural racism embedded in training data gets amplified at scale
- Opaque algorithms make it impossible to verify compliance with international humanitarian law
- False positives in threat detection can trigger escalation
- Historical conflict data bakes in past injustices

## 1.11 Economic Disruption from AI Causing Geopolitical Tension

**Key facts and developments:**

- The **IMF** projects that **40% of global jobs** could be affected by AI globally -- 60% in advanced economies, 42% in emerging markets, 26% in low-income countries.
- **UNDP** warns AI risks sparking a "new era of divergence" as development gaps between countries widen.
- A **2024 MDPI study** found a **65% probability** that AI-driven internal pressures could lead to increased social unrest, escalating into external conflicts; and a **70% probability** that increased nationalism and nationalistic movements will lead to greater geopolitical fragmentation.
- A **ScienceDirect paper (2026)** directly connects AI, economic inequality, and financial hurdles to sustainable peace.
- Countries that lead in AI development enjoy substantial advantages in productivity, innovation, and geopolitical influence -- reinforcing power asymmetries between the Global North and Global South.
- Developing countries offering IT services and call centers at lower cost could have their competitive edge wiped out as AI reduces labor intensity.

**Why it tips toward war:**
- Economic desperation creates conditions for conflict
- Growing inequality between nations fuels resentment and radicalization
- AI-displaced workers become recruitable for extremism
- Resource competition intensifies as AI changes which resources matter
- Economic warfare through AI/tech dominance becomes a new form of conflict

## 2.1 Near-Term Trajectories (2025-2035)

**Autonomous weapons proliferation:**
- AI-enabled drones will become the primary weapon in most conflicts. Ukraine's plan to ramp up from 0.5% to 50% AI-guided drones by 2025 is a template other nations will follow.

- AI-powered cyber weapons will target critical civilian infrastructure as standard practice.

**Information warfare normalization:**
- Deepfakes will become indistinguishable from reality for most audiences.
- AI-generated propaganda will be personalized at individual level.
- Trust in all media will collapse further, creating "epistemic chaos."

**Regulation race:**
- UN treaty on LAWS expected by **2026-2027** but likely to lack enforcement teeth.
- EU AI Act military exemptions will remain a gap.
- US-China bilateral agreements on AI in nuclear C2 will be tested.

## 2.6 The Concept of "Flash Wars"

**Definition:** Flash wars are algorithmic conflicts that escalate faster than humans can react -- analogous to flash crashes in financial markets caused by high-frequency trading algorithms.

**Key analysis:**

- **UNODA** has cautioned against flash wars in which algorithmic escalation intensifies a crisis before humans can intervene.
- **University of Pennsylvania Center for Ethics and the Rule of Law (CERL)** published "Preventing a Flash War: Countering the Risk of AI-Driven Escalation on the Battlefield."
- When **multiple AI-driven decision systems interact**, rapid recursive decision-making can trigger unpredictable conflict dynamics.
- The **2024 documentary "Flash Wars"** explored these scenarios in detail, featuring interviews with military and AI experts.
- **80,000 Hours podcast** with Paul Scharre discussed how AI could transform the nature of war through speed of engagement.

**The analogy:** Just as high-frequency trading moved too fast for human traders (the 2010 Flash Crash wiped $1 trillion in market value in minutes), warfare may reach a tipping point where human reaction times are a liability.

## 3.1 Use of AI in the Ukraine-Russia War

**AI-enabled drones -- Ukraine side:**
- Ukraine trained publicly available AI models on **classified battlefield data**, increasing hit rates **3-4x** according to think tank reports.
- In **2024**, Ukraine manufactured over **1.5 million FPV drones**; of ~2 million drones contracted, only 10,000 (0.5%) used AI guidance. The plan for **2025**: ramp up to **50% AI-guided drones**.

- When Ukrainian forces could strike **30-40km behind enemy lines** using AI-guided drones, Russian artillery fire dropped as essential networks were broken.

- Russia is equipping **armored trains with AI vision** to counter Ukrainian drone attacks.

**AI in reconnaissance and intelligence:**
- AI systems process satellite imagery, drone footage, and signals intelligence to identify targets in real time.
- Both sides use AI for electronic warfare, jamming, and counter-jamming.

## 3.2 Use of AI in the Israel-Gaza Conflict

**The Lavender System:**
- An AI-powered database that listed **37,000 Palestinian men** linked by AI to Hamas or PIJ.
- Uses machine learning to assign Gaza residents a **numerical "likelihood" score** of being an armed group member.
- After a sample was found to have a **90% accuracy rate**, the IDF gave sweeping approval for officers to adopt Lavender's kill lists.
- Human personnel often served as a **"rubber stamp"**, approving targets in as little as **20 seconds**, with no requirement to examine the raw intelligence data.

**The Gospel (Habsora) System:**
- AI system that determines which targets the Israeli Air Force would bomb.
- Automatically provides targeting recommendations to human analysts who decide whether to pass them to soldiers.
- Dramatically increased the pace of target generation compared to previous conflicts.

**"Where's Daddy?" System:**
- Tracked individuals identified by Lavender and alerted the military when they entered their family homes.
- This led to strikes on residential buildings, maximizing civilian casualties.

**Human Rights Concerns:**
- **Human Rights Watch** (September 2024): Published detailed questions and answers on Israel's use of digital tools in Gaza.
- **UN experts** (April 2024) deplored the use of AI to commit "domicide" in Gaza and called for a reparative approach.
- **972 Magazine** published the initial detailed investigation into the Lavender system (April 2024).

- The speed of AI engagement outpacing human override capability
